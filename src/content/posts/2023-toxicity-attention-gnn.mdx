## Faster compound screening

Toxicity prediction matters in drug discovery and chemical safety. Traditional LD50 testing can be reliable, but it is costly, slow, and ethically difficult. This project explores graph neural networks as a faster and more scalable way to screen compounds without relying on animal testing.

Molecules are represented as graphs: atoms become nodes and chemical bonds become edges. Attention layers can prioritize the atoms and bonds that matter for a prediction, while differentiable pooling captures molecular structure hierarchically.

The work combines attention-based message passing with differentiable pooling to represent molecular structure at multiple levels.

The results expose a familiar modeling challenge. Training loss falls well below test and validation loss, suggesting overfitting; dropout and batch normalization did not fully remove it. The original paper goes into the implementation and code in much more detail.
