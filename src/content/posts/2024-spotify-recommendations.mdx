## The project

This is an ongoing exploration of song recommendation systems built from Spotify data. The goal is to test multiple approaches, compare their recommendations, and make the trade-offs visible rather than treating a recommendation as a black box.

## A simple approach

The first experiment uses a dataset of song metadata. Alongside artist and year, each track has Spotify features such as acousticness, danceability, energy, instrumentalness, liveness, loudness, speechiness, and tempo. I percentile-normalized the values and compared tracks with Euclidean distance and cosine similarity.

The result was poor: the nearest songs to *Yesterday* were not meaningfully similar. That motivated the second approach.

## Neural network approach — Skip-gram embeddings

Spotify’s million-playlist dataset offers a different signal. Treat each song as a token and each playlist as a sentence, then train a Skip-gram model to predict the context of a song. Songs that occur together repeatedly should land near each other in the embedding space.

K-means clustering, elbow/silhouette analysis, and t-SNE visualizations gave a useful but imperfect view. The clusters flowed into one another, yet nearest-neighbor results were much more specific: Beatles tracks clustered with other Beatles songs, while less popular songs found stylistically related artists.
